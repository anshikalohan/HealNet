# Official Medical Guidelines

## Dengue Fever
Dengue is a mosquito-borne viral infection causing a severe flu-like illness.
**Symptoms:** High fever, severe headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pains, nausea, vomiting, swollen glands or rash.
**Prevention:** Avoid mosquito bites by using repellents, wearing long-sleeved clothes, and removing stagnant water around the home.
**When to seek help:** Seek immediate medical care if warning signs like severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, or lethargy appear within 24-48 hours after fever declines.

## Malaria
Malaria is a life-threatening disease caused by parasites that are transmitted to people through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes.
**Symptoms:** Fever, chills, and flu-like illness. Left untreated, they may develop severe complications and die.
**Prevention:** Use insecticide-treated mosquito nets and indoor residual spraying. Antimalarial medicines can also be used for prevention.
**Treatment:** Requires prompt medical diagnosis and treatment with prescription antimalarial drugs.

## COVID-19
COVID-19 is an infectious disease caused by the SARS-CoV-2 virus.
**Symptoms:** Fever, cough, fatigue, loss of taste or smell, shortness of breath, muscle aches.
**Prevention:** Stay up to date with vaccines, wear a well-fitting mask in crowded indoor spaces, practice good hand hygiene, and improve ventilation.
**When to seek help:** Seek emergency medical attention for trouble breathing, persistent pain or pressure in the chest, new confusion, or inability to wake or stay awake.

## Diabetes
Diabetes is a chronic disease that occurs either when the pancreas does not produce enough insulin or when the body cannot effectively use the insulin it produces.
**Symptoms:** Excessive thirst, frequent urination, unexplained weight loss, fatigue, blurred vision.
**Management:** Healthy diet, regular physical activity, maintaining a normal body weight, and avoiding tobacco use. Medication (insulin or oral agents) is often required.
**Screening:** Regular screening is recommended, especially for those over 35 or with risk factors like obesity or family history.

## Hypertension (High Blood Pressure)
Hypertension is a condition in which the blood vessels have persistently raised pressure. It is a major cause of premature death worldwide.
**Symptoms:** Often called a "silent killer" because it may show no symptoms. Some may experience morning headaches, nosebleeds, irregular heart rhythms, vision changes, or buzzing in the ears.
**Management:** Reducing salt intake, eating more fruit and vegetables, regular physical activity, avoiding tobacco, reducing alcohol consumption, and managing stress. Medication may be prescribed.

## Tuberculosis (TB)
Tuberculosis is an infectious disease usually caused by Mycobacterium tuberculosis bacteria. It generally affects the lungs.
**Symptoms:** Prolonged cough (more than 2-3 weeks), chest pain, coughing up blood or sputum, weakness or fatigue, weight loss, fever, and night sweats.
**Prevention and Treatment:** TB is preventable and curable. Treatment involves a standard 6-month course of four antimicrobial drugs. BCG vaccination helps prevent severe forms of TB in children.
